\title{AgentDataset: Assisted Synthesis of Tabular Data from Published Statistics}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{abstract}
We present AgentDataset, a command-line system that turns a research query into a synthetic
tabular dataset by discovering candidate documents, extracting marginals and pairwise
correlations with a large language model, reviewing the resulting claims, synthesizing
correlated rows through a Gaussian copula, and validating the output. The system is assisted
rather than autonomous: an LLM agent accepts, rejects, or defers every extracted claim against
its evidence, and a human may override those decisions. Every artifact carries a provenance
manifest. We reframe the internal fidelity score as a sampler-consistency diagnostic rather
than evidence of source fidelity, and show with a controlled corruption probe that it is blind
to upstream parameter error. On three real papers the deployed extractor reaches .304 micro-F1
on prespecified statistics. Across four public datasets, synthetic tables preserve marginals
and pairwise monotone association and retain much of the downstream utility of real data.
The system does not recover higher-order dependence, and aggregate-only input is not a privacy
mechanism. These results do not support unattended scientific curation or a privacy mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Published papers routinely report the aggregates needed to reconstruct a plausible population:
means, standard deviations, category probabilities, and pairwise correlations. Record-level data
are often unavailable for privacy, contractual, or licensing reasons, and record-trained
synthesizers such as SDV \citep{patki2016} require individual rows that this setting does not
provide. AgentDataset automates the translation from published aggregates into a usable table
while keeping source selection and claim approval reviewable.

The contributions are: (1) an end-to-end assisted workflow exposed as a CLI, with an agentic
review gate and a human override; (2) a copula-based synthesizer with correlation diagnostics and
an opt-in nearest-PSD repair; (3) an evidence-aware reconciliation module that flags and separates
incompatible cross-source measures; and (4) an honest validation story that separates sampler
consistency from source fidelity and states an explicit operating envelope. The evaluation is
designed around questions of interest to AI-for-science research: can the pipeline find and read
real papers, how much utility survives, and what do the internal scores actually certify?

\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Discovery and extraction}
\label{sec:extract}

\texttt{optimize\_query()} expands the user query with an LLM; a search API returns ranked
candidates that are deduplicated by normalized URL and scored for statistical availability.
HTML is converted with trafilatura and PDFs with a layout-aware parser. LLM extraction from
scientific text is a growing area \citep{nasar2018,liu2025,song2025,sror2026}, but reported
failure modes include hallucinated numbers and context errors. Extraction is LLM-only:
long documents are chunked (8000 characters, 500 overlap), each chunk is parsed with a
natural-language prompt that enforces the output schema, and results are merged by normalized
variable name and correlation pair. Each claim carries an evidence passage, source identity,
and an evidence type (reported, derived, or inferred). Unresolvable names and unsupported values
are preserved as claims rather than silently dropped.

\subsection{Agentic review and reconciliation}
\label{sec:review}

Before synthesis, an LLM agent accepts, rejects, or defers every claim against its evidence and
the declared population, arm, and time point; only accepted claims enter the specification. A
human can override agent decisions through the \texttt{review} command. When several sources are
selected, the shipped run path still averages same-named variables and correlation pairs. A
separate reconciliation module groups measures by normalized name, unit, and study context,
keeps per-study estimates side by side, and records an explicit decision for each merge; values
from incompatible populations remain separate. Wiring that module into the default workflow is
ongoing work.

\subsection{Synthesis}
\label{sec:synth}

The synthesizer draws parametric marginals (normal, uniform, gamma, categorical) and imposes
pairwise correlations via a rank transform over a latent Gaussian
\citep{joe2014,trivedi2006}; vine-copula alternatives \citep{aas2009} need richer reported
statistics than a typical paper provides. Diagnostics record declared,
applied, and skipped pairs plus matrix conditioning. Positive-definiteness is measured by
eigendecomposition, not inferred from a Cholesky failure. An opt-in nearest-PSD repair is
available, with the unmodified matrix retained. Categorical variables are drawn as integer codes
and rank-transformed; treating arbitrary label order as ordinal is a documented limitation.

\subsection{Validation and the agentic loop}
\label{sec:loop}

The validator reports KS (or chi-square for categorical) fit, correlation similarity, bias, and a
synthetic-row dispersion diagnostic. Plausibility, grounding, and claim-grounding scores feed a
trust score, and an LLM critique suggests the next action. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pivot} summarizes
the noise-pivot loop; $\eta$ is the noise level, $p$ patience, $\alpha$ the exploitation factor,
$\beta$ the exploration multiplier, $\rho$ the reset interval, and $p\rho$ the reset multiple.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Synthesis--validation--critique loop.}
\label{alg:pivot}
\textbf{Input}: reviewed specification $S$, iterations $T$, initial noise $\eta_0$ \\
\textbf{Parameter}: $\eta_{\min}$, $\eta_{\max}$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\rho$, $p\rho$ \\
\textbf{Output}: best table and its report
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE $\eta \leftarrow \eta_0$; $b \leftarrow -\infty$; $u \leftarrow 0$
\FOR{$t = 1$ to $T$}
  \STATE $D \leftarrow \textsc{Synthesize}(S, \eta)$
  \STATE $r \leftarrow \textsc{Validate}(D, S, \text{evidence})$
  \IF{$r.\text{score} > b$}
    \STATE $b \leftarrow r.\text{score}$; save artifacts; $u \leftarrow 0$
  \ELSE
    \STATE $u \leftarrow u + 1$
    \IF{$u \bmod (p\rho) = 0$}
      \STATE $\eta \leftarrow \eta_0$
    \ELSIF{$u \bmod p = 0$}
      \STATE $\eta \leftarrow \max(\eta_{\min}, \alpha\eta)$
    \ELSE
      \STATE $\eta \leftarrow \min(\eta_{\max}, \beta\eta)$
    \ENDIF
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $a \leftarrow \textsc{Critique}(r, \text{evidence})$
  \STATE halt if $a = \text{stop}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{return} best saved table
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Evaluation questions}
\label{sec:questions}

All live calls were routed through OpenRouter via \texttt{litellm}; the default extraction model
is \texttt{openrouter/openai/gpt-4o} and the agent-loop model is \texttt{openrouter/auto}. Seeds
control data splits and synthesis, and reported values are means over seeds unless noted.

\begin{description}
\item[Q1: Real documents.] Three heterogeneous papers (a randomized trial, an NHANES
  comparison, and a student mental-health survey) are processed end to end; discovery, parsing,
  extraction, and synthesis are scored separately over 30 synthesis seeds.
\item[Q2: Downstream utility.] Adult, Pima, German Credit, and Concrete are evaluated over 30
  paired splits using gold summaries computed from each training fold, so extraction is held
  perfect. Logistic regression, gradient-boosted trees, one-shot synthesis, independent
  marginals, and an SDV Gaussian copula fitted on real records are compared.
\item[Q3: Extraction.] A 30-case challenge set covers ten failure categories with five
  repetitions per case and arm. Prompt style and model are varied; precision, recall, F1,
  hallucination, abstention, tokens, and cost are measured.
\item[Q4: Optimization.] An eight-domain $\times$ five-seed $\times$ six-budget sweep yields
  240 cells with a fresh synthesizer per cell, plus a noise-matched one-shot control and paired
  tests of zero versus five iterations.
\item[Q5: Cost.] Per-call cost and latency are augmented with instrumented end-to-end runs over
  the real-paper pipeline, separating API, network, and local time.
\item[Q6: Diagnostics.] Controlled parameter corruption, correlation-matrix stress tests, and
  Adult calibration studies probe what the internal score certifies.
\end{description}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Q1: Real papers}
\label{sec:q1}

Discovery found all three prespecified sources; the CALERIE trial first appears at rank 13, so
this is target recovery rather than recall@5. On the prespecified fields, LLM extraction reaches
precision .222, recall .480, and .304 micro-F1, while the regex baseline extracts none of them.
The three-paper extraction replicate cost \$0.0343 and took 13.9 seconds. Synthesis from LLM
extractions approaches synthesis from hand-curated gold statistics on the trial and the survey,
but the coarse categorical NHANES specification remains hard even with gold inputs
(Table~\ref{tab:realpapers} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). Extraction is the bottleneck, not
synthesis. Figure~\ref{fig:walkthrough} shows one end-to-end example.

\subsection{Q2: Downstream utility}
\label{sec:q2}

With gold training-fold summaries, AgentDataset tracks SDV closely on the three classification
tasks (for example, Adult accuracy .799 for both) and beats independent marginals wherever
dependence matters. The one-shot copula is nearly as good as the loop, so most of the benefit
comes from the reported correlations rather than refinement. On Concrete regression, SDV is
clearly better (R$^2$ .622 versus .528), which is expected because it sees record-level data and
can model nonlinear structure beyond the reported pairwise correlations
(Table~\ref{tab:utility}). Threshold selection on synthetic validation data recovers most of the
Adult minority-F1 gap without using real test labels, but cannot restore ranking loss
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration}).

\subsection{Q3: Extraction}
\label{sec:q3}

The compressed Caveman prompt saves billed prompt tokens but lowers quality: on the challenge
set its F1 is .710 versus .778 for the schema-matched natural-language prompt, with hallucination
rising from .098 to .154. Regex reaches F1 .156 and is retained only as a documented, near-silent
baseline. Variable-name accuracy stays in the 0.44--0.56 range, so value extraction is much
easier than assigning the correct variable identity. Provider-level cost and latency are small
but non-trivial (Table~\ref{tab:provider}).

\subsection{Q4: Optimization budget}
\label{sec:q4}

The jump from zero to three iterations is largely a noise-level effect: a noise-matched one-shot
control at the loop's starting noise scores 95.82, identical to the one-iteration arm, while
three iterations add 4.02 fidelity points (paired $t$ $p=0.0034$). In a wider schedule sweep, a
fixed noise of 0.01 beats the shipped adaptive schedule at the same budget (99.88 versus 88.50),
and the reset branch is inert at the shipped settings. The defaults are retained for
reproducibility; iteration count is a weak substitute for a well-chosen starting noise
(Tables~\ref{tab:noise} and~\ref{tab:pivot}).

\subsection{Q5: Cost and latency}
\label{sec:q5}

One finished dataset averages \$0.01686 and 41.84 seconds. Of that, 33.06 seconds is network
time, 7.80 seconds is LLM API time across three calls (query expansion, source suggestion, and
extraction), and under one second is local computation. Human source-review time is excluded.
Discovery, not statistical modelling, dominates cost and latency (Table~\ref{tab:e2e}).

\subsection{Q6: What the score certifies}
\label{sec:q6}

Perturbing declared parameters before synthesis leaves reported fidelity, correlation, and KS
scores unchanged at every corruption level, while external KS distance against real data rises
from 0.13 to 0.97. The score checks whether the sampler followed its specification, not whether
the specification was correct. Correlation diagnostics do carry signal about synthesis failures:
name resolution, not matrix non-PD, was the dominant way declared dependence was lost on real
inputs, and the repaired path applies all resolvable pairs (Tables~\ref{tab:validity}
and~\ref{tab:correlation}). Repair cannot recover information that was never declared.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

AgentDataset reproduces per-variable marginals and pairwise monotone association, and nothing
beyond that: no higher-order dependence, no defensible categorical dependence model, and no
formal privacy guarantee. Aggregate-only input follows a long tradition of disclosure control
\citep{hundepool2012,rubin1993,taub2018} but does not itself establish anonymity. Internal fidelity is a sampler-consistency diagnostic; trust and
external utility tests carry the evidential weight. Extraction is reliable on explicitly stated
numbers but weak on variable identity, scope, and abstention. Cost figures exclude human review,
and results are bounded by the four evaluated datasets and three papers. Distribution shift
between a published summary and a target population remains an open risk. Validation against
independent evidence and use of the reconciliation module as the default merge path are the next
steps.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an assisted, auditable path from published statistics to synthetic data, with
explicit operating limits and result-backed evaluation. The system is useful where record-level
data cannot be shared but aggregate statistics are public, provided its claims are read at the
narrow scope supported by the evidence.

\title{AgentDataset: Assisted Synthesis of Tabular Data from Published Statistics}
\author{Anonymous Submission}

\begin{abstract}
AgentDataset turns a research query into a synthetic tabular dataset: it discovers
documents, extracts marginals and pairwise correlations with a language model,
optionally reviews each claim against its evidence, synthesizes correlated rows
through a Gaussian copula, and validates the result. Source selection and claim
approval remain reviewable by a human, and artifacts carry provenance manifests.
We evaluate seven questions on real papers and four public datasets. The deployed
extractor reaches .304 micro-F1 on prespecified statistics from three papers.
Synthetic tables preserve marginals and pairwise monotone association but lose
minority-class and nonlinear utility. A corruption probe shows the internal
fidelity score is blind to upstream parameter error; a dedicated study shows the
review gate rejects all unsupported claims yet accepts only 3 of 20 verified ones.
These results support an assisted, auditable workflow, not unattended scientific
curation or a privacy mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Published papers routinely report the aggregates needed to reconstruct a plausible
population: means, standard deviations, category probabilities, and pairwise
correlations. Record-level data are often unavailable for privacy, contractual, or
licensing reasons, and record-trained synthesizers such as SDV~\citep{patki2016}
require individual rows that this setting does not provide. AgentDataset automates
the translation from published aggregates into a usable table while keeping source
selection and claim approval reviewable.

This paper makes four contributions: (1) an end-to-end assisted workflow exposed as
a CLI, with an agentic review gate and a human override; (2) a copula synthesizer
with correlation diagnostics and an opt-in nearest-PSD repair; (3) an evidence-aware
reconciliation module that flags incomparable cross-source measures; and (4) an
evaluation that separates sampler consistency from source fidelity and states an
explicit operating envelope. Two findings are deliberately negative: the internal
score cannot detect upstream parameter error, and the current review gate is too
conservative to be the default. The evaluation is organized around questions of
interest to AI-for-science research: can the pipeline find and read real papers,
how much downstream utility survives, and what do the internal scores certify?

\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Discovery and extraction}
\label{sec:extract}

\texttt{optimize\_query()} expands the user query with an LLM; a search API returns
ranked candidates that are deduplicated by normalized URL and scored for
statistical availability. HTML is converted with trafilatura and PDFs with a
layout-aware parser. LLM extraction from scientific text is an active research
area~\citep{nasar2018,liu2025,song2025,sror2026}, but reported failure modes include
hallucinated numbers and context errors. Extraction is LLM-only: long documents are
chunked (8000 characters, 500 overlap), each chunk is parsed with a natural-language
prompt that enforces the output schema, and results are merged by normalized
variable name and correlation pair. Each claim carries an evidence passage, source
identity, and an evidence type (reported, derived, or inferred). Unresolvable names
and unsupported values are preserved as claims rather than silently dropped.

\subsection{Agentic review and reconciliation}
\label{sec:review}

Before synthesis, an LLM agent accepts, rejects, or defers every claim against its
evidence and the declared population, arm, and time point; the \texttt{run} command
builds the specification only from accepted claims. A human can override agent
decisions through \texttt{review}. When several sources are selected, the shipped
run path still averages same-named variables and correlation pairs. A separate
reconciliation module groups measures by normalized name, unit, and study context,
keeps per-study estimates side by side, and records an explicit decision for each
merge; incompatible populations remain separate. Wiring that module into the
default workflow is ongoing work, and Section~\ref{sec:q7} measures the gate rather
than assuming it helps.

\subsection{Synthesis}
\label{sec:synth}

The synthesizer draws parametric marginals (normal, uniform, gamma, categorical)
and imposes pairwise correlations via a rank transform over a latent
Gaussian~\citep{joe2014,trivedi2006}; vine-copula alternatives~\citep{aas2009} need
richer reported statistics than a typical paper provides. Diagnostics record
declared, applied, and skipped pairs plus matrix conditioning. Positive-definiteness
is measured by eigendecomposition, not inferred from a Cholesky failure. An opt-in
nearest-PSD repair is available, with the unmodified matrix retained. Categorical
variables are drawn as integer codes and rank-transformed; treating arbitrary label
order as ordinal is a documented limitation.

\subsection{Validation and the agentic loop}
\label{sec:loop}

The validator reports KS (or chi-square for categorical) fit, correlation
similarity, bias, and a synthetic-row dispersion diagnostic. Plausibility,
grounding, and claim-grounding scores feed a trust score, and an LLM critique
suggests the next action. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pivot} summarizes the noise-pivot loop;
$\eta$ is the noise level, $p$ patience, $\alpha$ the exploitation factor, $\beta$
the exploration multiplier, $\rho$ the reset interval, and $p\rho$ the reset
multiple.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Synthesis--validation--critique loop.}
\label{alg:pivot}
\textbf{Input}: reviewed specification $S$, iterations $T$, initial noise $\eta_0$ \\
\textbf{Parameter}: $\eta_{\min}$, $\eta_{\max}$, $p$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\rho$, $p\rho$ \\
\textbf{Output}: best table and its report
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE $\eta \leftarrow \eta_0$; $b \leftarrow -\infty$; $u \leftarrow 0$
\FOR{$t = 1$ to $T$}
  \STATE $D \leftarrow \textsc{Synthesize}(S, \eta)$
  \STATE $r \leftarrow \textsc{Validate}(D, S, \text{evidence})$
  \IF{$r.\text{score} > b$}
    \STATE $b \leftarrow r.\text{score}$; save artifacts; $u \leftarrow 0$
  \ELSE
    \STATE $u \leftarrow u + 1$
    \IF{$u \bmod (p\rho) = 0$}
      \STATE $\eta \leftarrow \eta_0$
    \ELSIF{$u \bmod p = 0$}
      \STATE $\eta \leftarrow \max(\eta_{\min}, \alpha\eta)$
    \ELSE
      \STATE $\eta \leftarrow \min(\eta_{\max}, \beta\eta)$
    \ENDIF
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $a \leftarrow \textsc{Critique}(r, \text{evidence})$
  \STATE halt if $a = \text{stop}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{return} best saved table
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Evaluation questions}
\label{sec:questions}

All live calls were routed through OpenRouter via \texttt{litellm}; the default
extraction model is \texttt{openrouter/openai/gpt-4o} and the agent-loop model is
\texttt{openrouter/auto}. Seeds control data splits and synthesis, and reported
values are means over seeds unless noted. Results come in two tiers: Q1--Q6 are
frozen measurements of the extraction--synthesis core, while Q7 is a separate
study of the review and reconciliation stages. The two tiers are not mixed.

\begin{description}
\item[Q1: Real documents.] Three heterogeneous papers (a randomized trial, an
  NHANES comparison, and a student mental-health survey) are processed end to end;
  discovery, parsing, extraction, and synthesis are scored separately over 30
  synthesis seeds.
\item[Q2: Downstream utility.] Adult, Pima, German Credit, and Concrete are
  evaluated over 30 paired splits using gold summaries computed from each training
  fold, so extraction is held perfect. Logistic regression, gradient-boosted trees,
  one-shot synthesis, independent marginals, and an SDV Gaussian copula fitted on
  real records are compared.
\item[Q3: Extraction.] A 30-case challenge set covers ten failure categories with
  five repetitions per case and arm. Prompt style and model are varied; precision,
  recall, F1, hallucination, abstention, tokens, and cost are measured.
\item[Q4: Optimization.] An eight-domain $\times$ five-seed $\times$ six-budget
  sweep yields 240 cells with a fresh synthesizer per cell, plus a noise-matched
  one-shot control and paired tests of zero versus five iterations.
\item[Q5: Cost.] Per-call cost and latency are augmented with instrumented
  end-to-end runs over the real-paper pipeline, separating API, network, and local
  time.
\item[Q6: Diagnostics.] Controlled parameter corruption, correlation-matrix
  stress tests, and Adult calibration studies probe what the internal score
  certifies.
\item[Q7: Review gate.] A dedicated study runs the production reviewer on saved
  verified and unsupported claims and measures its effect on deterministic
  synthesis and cross-source reconciliation.
\end{description}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Q1: Real papers}
\label{sec:q1}

Discovery found all three prespecified sources; the CALERIE trial first appears at
rank 13, so this is target recovery rather than recall@5. On the prespecified
fields, LLM extraction reaches precision .222, recall .480, and .304 micro-F1, while
the regex baseline extracts none of them. The three-paper extraction replicate cost
\$0.0343 and took 13.9 seconds. Synthesis from LLM extractions approaches synthesis
from hand-curated gold statistics on the trial (97.41 vs 98.61 fidelity) and the
survey (96.81 vs 98.45), but the coarse categorical NHANES specification remains
hard even with gold inputs (61.63 vs 63.35; Table~\ref{tab:realpapers} in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). These runs use the extractor directly; the review gate
is evaluated separately in Section~\ref{sec:q7}. Extraction is the bottleneck, not
synthesis. Figure~\ref{fig:walkthrough} shows one end-to-end example.

\subsection{Q2: Downstream utility}
\label{sec:q2}

Table~\ref{tab:utility} reports minority-F1/ROC-AUC for classification and
R$^2$/RMSE for regression over 30 paired splits with gold training-fold summaries.
On Adult, AgentDataset reaches .381/.850 versus TRTR .542/.850 and SDV .112/.838;
independent marginals collapse to .000/.509. ROC-AUC matches TRTR and is close to
SDV, so much of the ranking signal survives, but minority F1 is far more sensitive
to the decision threshold. The one-shot copula is essentially identical (.382/.850),
which indicates that the reported correlations, rather than refinement, supply the
benefit. On Concrete, AgentDataset reaches R$^2$ .528 versus .622 for SDV, as
expected because SDV fits record-level data and captures nonlinear structure beyond
reported pairwise correlations. Selecting the threshold on synthetic validation data
raises Adult minority F1 from .381 to .617 without using real test labels, but cannot
remove the ranking deficit (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}).

\subsection{Q3: Extraction}
\label{sec:q3}

The compressed Caveman prompt saves billed prompt tokens but lowers quality: on the
challenge set its F1 is .710 versus .778 for the schema-matched natural-language
prompt, with hallucination rising from .098 to .154. Regex reaches F1 .156 and is
retained only as a documented, near-silent baseline. Variable-name accuracy stays
in the 0.44--0.56 range (\texttt{results/extraction\_challenge\_summary.csv}), so
value extraction is much easier than assigning the correct variable identity.
Provider-level cost and latency are small but non-trivial
(Table~\ref{tab:provider}).

\subsection{Q4: Optimization budget}
\label{sec:q4}

The jump from zero to three iterations is largely a noise-level effect. Budget zero
uses the standalone default noise of 0.1 and scores 59.82; the loop starts at 0.02.
A separate noise-matched one-shot control at 0.02 scores 95.82 over the same 40
(text, seed) cells (\texttt{results/one\_shot\_noise\_matched.csv}), identical to the
one-iteration arm in Table~\ref{tab:noise}; three iterations add 4.02 fidelity
points (paired $t$ $p=0.0034$). In a wider schedule sweep, a fixed noise of 0.01
beats the shipped adaptive schedule at the same budget (99.88 versus 88.50), and the
reset branch is inert at the shipped settings (Table~\ref{tab:pivot}). The defaults
are retained for reproducibility; iteration count is a weak substitute for a
well-chosen starting noise.

\subsection{Q5: Cost and latency}
\label{sec:q5}

One finished dataset averages \$0.01686 and 41.84 seconds. Of that, 33.06 seconds is
network time, 7.80 seconds is LLM API time across three calls (query expansion,
source suggestion, and extraction), and under one second is local computation. Human
source-review time is excluded. Discovery, not statistical modelling, dominates cost
and latency (Table~\ref{tab:e2e}).

\subsection{Q6: What the score certifies}
\label{sec:q6}

Perturbing declared parameters before synthesis leaves reported fidelity,
correlation, and KS scores unchanged at every corruption level, while external KS
distance against real data rises from 0.13 to 0.97. The score checks whether the
sampler followed its specification, not whether the specification was correct.
Correlation diagnostics do carry signal about synthesis failures: name resolution,
not matrix non-PD, was the dominant way declared dependence was lost on real inputs,
and the repaired path applies all resolvable pairs
(Tables~\ref{tab:validity} and~\ref{tab:correlation}). Repair cannot recover information that was never declared.

\subsection{Q7: Review gate and reconciliation}
\label{sec:q7}

The frozen benchmarks above bypass the review stage, so we ran a dedicated study of
the gate itself. From three source documents we built 55 claims: 20 statistics from
the dual-pass gold standard with real evidence passages, and 35 recorded unsupported
extractions with none. The production reviewer rejected all 35 unsupported claims
(100\%) but accepted only 3 of the 20 verified claims, deferring 12 and rejecting 5.
Filtering to reviewed claims therefore removes unsupported statistics at the cost of
discarding most verified ones: two of the three sources end with no synthesizable
variables (Table~\ref{tab:review_gate}). The \texttt{gold} row there is a reduced
specification built only from numeric marginal and correlation rows of the ground
truth file and is synthesized once at fixed noise; its fidelity values are not
comparable to the looped 30-seed values in Q1. Reconciliation of the three gold
specifications kept the one shared variable (\texttt{age}) separate rather than
averaging across incompatible studies. These are mechanism and calibration results,
not evidence that review improves downstream utility.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

AgentDataset reproduces per-variable marginals and pairwise monotone association,
and nothing beyond that: no higher-order dependence, no defensible categorical
dependence model, and no formal privacy guarantee. Aggregate-only input follows a
long tradition of disclosure control~\citep{hundepool2012,rubin1993,taub2018} but
does not itself establish anonymity. Internal fidelity is a sampler-consistency
diagnostic; trust and external utility tests carry the evidential weight. Extraction
is reliable on explicitly stated numbers but weak on variable identity, scope, and
abstention. The review gate rejects unsupported claims reliably but discards most
verified ones, so prompt calibration and the human override are required before it
can be the default. Cost figures exclude human review, and results are bounded by
the four evaluated datasets and three papers. Distribution shift between a published
summary and a target population remains an open risk. Validation against independent
evidence and use of the reconciliation module as the default merge path are the next
steps.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an assisted, auditable path from published statistics to synthetic data,
with explicit operating limits and two tiers of evidence: frozen measurements of the
statistical core and a separate study of the review and reconciliation stages. The
system is useful where record-level data cannot be shared but aggregate statistics
are public, provided its claims are read at the narrow scope supported by those
measurements.
